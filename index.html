About Math Bridge Hub

Math Bridge Hub is a mathematics learning, development and networking community created to connect people through mathematics and demonstrate how mathematical knowledge can be applied beyond the classroom. We bring together scholars, educators, mathematicians, researchers and professionals to learn, share ideas, develop skills, collaborate on meaningful projects and discover how mathematics contributes to science, technology, business, education and society. Our ambition is to grow Math Bridge Hub into a strong mathematical community where knowledge is shared, relationships are built and mathematics becomes a bridge to real opportunities and real-world solutions.

Our Vision

To build a strong and connected mathematical community where scholars, educators, researchers and professionals use mathematics to create knowledge, opportunities and solutions for the real world.

Our Mission

To provide accessible mathematics learning, workshops, mentorship, networking, research exposure and practical experiences that help people develop mathematical knowledge and apply it meaningfully.

Our Ambition

We aim to develop Math Bridge Hub into a growing mathematics network that connects education, research, careers, technology, innovation and society through mathematics.

Building More Than a Learning Platform

Math Bridge Hub is being developed with a long-term vision. We do not want mathematics to remain only in textbooks, classrooms and examinations. We want to create a community where mathematical knowledge can lead to conversations, collaboration, mentorship, research, professional development and practical solutions.

Our ambition is to connect people who love mathematics with people who use mathematics. Through learning resources, workshops and networking, Math Bridge Hub will provide a space where ideas can move from theory to practice and where relationships can grow into meaningful collaborations.

📚 Primary Mathematics — Grade 1–6

Building strong mathematical foundations through numbers, operations, measurement, geometry, fractions, data and problem solving.

Grade 1 Mathematics

Numbers and Counting

Numbers tell us how many objects there are. Counting means assigning numbers to objects in order.

For example: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5

If there are 7 books, the number of books is 7.

Practice

  1. Count from 1 to 20.
  2. Which is greater: 8 or 5?
  3. What number comes after 14?
Addition

Addition means combining quantities. The symbol is +.

3 + 4 = 7

Practice

  1. 2 + 5 = ?
  2. 4 + 6 = ?
  3. 7 + 8 = ?
Subtraction

Subtraction means taking one quantity away from another.

10 − 4 = 6
Shapes and Patterns
  • Triangle — three sides.
  • Square — four equal sides.
  • Rectangle — four sides.
  • Circle — curved boundary.
2, 4, 6, 8, ... The pattern increases by 2.

Grade 2 Mathematics

Place Value

Place value tells us the value represented by a digit according to its position.

347 = 300 + 40 + 7

3 represents hundreds, 4 represents tens and 7 represents ones.

Multiplication

Multiplication can be understood as repeated addition.

4 × 3 = 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 12
Division

Division means sharing a quantity into equal groups.

12 ÷ 3 = 4
Fractions

A fraction represents part of a whole.

1/2 means one part out of two equal parts.

Grade 3 Mathematics

Fractions

A fraction has a numerator and denominator.

3/4 3 = numerator 4 = denominator

The denominator tells us how many equal parts make the whole, while the numerator tells us how many parts are considered.

Measurement
  • Length — metres and centimetres.
  • Mass — kilograms and grams.
  • Capacity — litres and millilitres.
  • Time — seconds, minutes and hours.
1 metre = 100 centimetres.
Money
Price = KSh 70 Amount paid = KSh 100 Change = 100 − 70 = KSh 30

Grade 4 Mathematics

Whole Numbers

Whole numbers include zero and positive counting numbers.

0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, ...
Decimals
0.5 = 5/10 = 1/2
Perimeter
P = 2(l + w)
l = 8 cm w = 3 cm P = 2(8 + 3) P = 22 cm

Grade 5 Mathematics

Percentages
Percentage = (Part / Whole) × 100%
25/100 × 100% = 25%
Ratio

A ratio compares two quantities.

4 boys and 6 girls: 4 : 6 = 2 : 3
Area
Area of rectangle = length × width
8 × 5 = 40 cm²

Grade 6 Mathematics

Fractions, Decimals and Percentages
1/2 = 0.5 = 50%

These are three different representations of the same quantity.

Ratio and Proportion
2 : 3 = 4 : 6
Data Handling

Data is information collected for analysis. It can be represented using tables, bar graphs, line graphs and pie charts.

Algebraic Expressions

Transformations

Probability

Statistics

Grade 9 Mathematics

Pythagoras' Theorem
a² + b² = c²
3² + 4² = c² 9 + 16 = 25 c = 5
Trigonometry
sin θ = opposite / hypotenuse
cos θ = adjacent / hypotenuse

Coordinate Geometry

📗 Senior Secondary Mathematics — Grade 10–12

Develop deeper mathematical reasoning for university, science, technology, engineering, economics and other careers.

Grade 10 Mathematics

Functions
f(x) = 2x + 3 f(4) = 2(4) + 3 f(4) = 11
Quadratic Equations
ax² + bx + c = 0

Quadratic formula:

x = [-b ± √(b² − 4ac)] / 2a
x² − 5x + 6 = 0 (x − 2)(x − 3) = 0 x = 2 or x = 3
Sequences
aₙ = a + (n − 1)d
2, 5, 8, 11, ... d = 3.

Grade 11 Mathematics

Vectors

A vector has magnitude and direction.

|v| = √(a² + b²)
v = (3,4) |v| = 5
Financial Mathematics
I = PRT
P = 10,000 R = 5% = 0.05 T = 2 I = 1,000
Probability

Probability ranges from 0 to 1.

  • 0 — impossible.
  • 1 — certain.
  • Between 0 and 1 — possible events.

🎓 University Mathematics

Advanced mathematics resources covering major areas studied at undergraduate and postgraduate level.

University Calculus

Limits

A limit describes the value a function approaches as its input approaches a particular value.

lim x→2 (x + 3) = 5
Differentiation
d/dx(xⁿ) = nxⁿ⁻¹
d/dx(x⁴) = 4x³
Integration
∫xⁿdx = xⁿ⁺¹/(n+1) + C
∫x²dx = x³/3 + C

Linear Algebra

Matrices

A matrix is a rectangular arrangement of numbers.

A = [ 1 2 ] [ 3 4 ]
Determinants
det(A) = ad − bc
Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors
Av = λv

Eigenvalues and eigenvectors are important in differential equations, data science, physics and numerical mathematics.

Real Analysis

Sequences and Convergence

A sequence converges when its terms approach a fixed value.

aₙ = 1/n As n → ∞: aₙ → 0
Continuity
lim x→a f(x) = f(a)
Mathematical Proof
  • Direct proof
  • Contradiction
  • Contrapositive
  • Mathematical induction

Abstract Algebra

Groups

A group is a set with an operation satisfying closure, associativity, identity and inverse properties.

Rings and Fields

Rings and fields are important algebraic structures used throughout modern mathematics.

Functional Analysis

Normed Spaces

A norm gives a mathematical measure of the size or length of an element.

||x||
Banach Spaces

A Banach space is a complete normed vector space.

Hilbert Spaces

A Hilbert space is a complete inner-product space.

Graph Theory

Graphs

A graph consists of vertices and edges. Graphs can represent roads, communication networks, social networks and computer systems.

Paths and Cycles

A path is a sequence of connected vertices. A cycle is a closed path.

Trees

A tree is a connected graph with no cycles.

Number Theory

Prime Numbers

A prime number has exactly two positive divisors: 1 and itself.

2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, ...
Modular Arithmetic
17 mod 5 = 2
a ≡ b (mod n)

Ordinary Differential Equations

First-Order Differential Equations
y′ = y dy/y = dx ln|y| = x + C y = Ceˣ
Second-Order Differential Equations
y″ + 5y′ + 6y = 0

Characteristic equation:

r² + 5r + 6 = 0

Therefore:

r = −2, −3

General solution:

y = C₁e⁻²ᵗ + C₂e⁻³ᵗ
Initial Value Problems
y′ = y y(0) = 1 y = eᵗ

University Statistics

Descriptive Statistics
Mean = Σx/n

Other measures include median, mode, variance and standard deviation.

Probability Distributions
  • Binomial distribution
  • Poisson distribution
  • Normal distribution
  • Uniform distribution
Hypothesis Testing

Hypothesis testing uses sample data to make conclusions about a population.

Numerical Methods

Euler's Method
yₙ₊₁ = yₙ + h f(tₙ,yₙ)
For y′ = y, y₀ = 1, h = 0.1: y₁ = 1 + 0.1(1) y₁ = 1.1
Runge-Kutta Methods

Runge-Kutta methods estimate solutions of differential equations using several evaluations of the derivative within each step. Common methods include RK2 and RK4.

Root-Finding Methods
  • Bisection method
  • Newton-Raphson method
  • Secant method

Mathematics in the Real World & Why Mathematics Matters

Mathematics is more than numbers, formulas and calculations. It is a language for understanding patterns, analysing information, solving problems, making decisions and describing the world around us. From the technology we use every day to engineering, medicine, finance, economics, science and environmental studies, mathematics plays a fundamental role in modern life. Math Bridge Hub seeks to help scholars understand not only how to do mathematics, but also why mathematics matters, where it is used and how mathematical thinking can contribute to solving real-world problems.

Mathematics in Everyday Life

Mathematics helps us manage time, money, measurements, planning, comparisons and everyday decisions.

Finance & Business

Mathematics is used for interest, loans, investments, budgets, profit, risk analysis and financial planning.

Engineering

Engineers use algebra, calculus, geometry, differential equations and numerical methods to design systems.

Technology & AI

Mathematics supports computer graphics, algorithms, cryptography, artificial intelligence and data science.

Medicine

Mathematics and statistics help researchers analyse data, model diseases and evaluate treatments.

Economics

Mathematical models help economists understand markets, growth, prices and resource allocation.

Science

Mathematics provides models and methods used to describe physical, biological and natural phenomena.

Environment

Mathematical models can help study population growth, climate systems and environmental changes.

Decision-Making

Mathematical reasoning helps us analyse information, compare alternatives and make informed decisions.
